Atkinson Hyperlegible

Body text set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, headings in Inter Tight, labels in JetBrains Mono. This is the proposed stack for readers of all ages, including low-vision users. Review it on the device and screen size your customers actually use.

Primary action

Why this font for all ages

Atkinson Hyperlegible was designed by the Braille Institute to make letters that are easy to mistake for one another clearly distinct. That helps older readers, low-vision readers, and anyone glancing at a small label on a phone in bright sunlight. For everyone else it still reads as a clean, neutral sans-serif, so nothing feels clinical or unusual.

Headings stay in Inter Tight so the Pattadar brand voice is unchanged. Only the body copy switches, which is where reading comfort matters most.

Body text at a real reading length

A pattadar is the recorded holder of agricultural land. When a family reviews their holdings on this platform, they are often doing it together, across three generations, on whatever phone happens to be in the room. The text has to survive a scratched screen, a bright verandah, and eyes that no longer focus the way they did at twenty. Good letterforms do quiet work here: they let someone read a survey number aloud without squinting, and they keep a long paragraph from turning into a grey wall.

Notice the spacing between words and the height of the lines. At this line height a reader can move to the next line without losing their place, which is one of the first things that becomes hard with age or low vision. Nothing here shouts. The page simply gets out of the way of the words.
